Classic Thanksgiving Stuffing Recipe with Herbs and Celery

Make classic oven-baked stuffing with dried bread, onion, celery, butter and herbs, plus practical make-ahead and food-safety tips.

Publication Today EditorialPublished November 17, 2026

At a glance

Side dish · About 1 hour 20 minutes including bread preparation

Ingredients

  • 10–12 cups dried bread cubes
  • 6 tbsp butter
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 celery ribs, diced
  • 2 tsp dried sage
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • ½ tsp dried rosemary
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 2–2½ cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley

Golden, savoury bread stuffing

This classic bread stuffing is baked in its own dish for a crisp top and tender centre. It serves about 8 and uses dried bread cubes, onion, celery, butter and familiar herbs. Baking separately makes it easier to check doneness and serve alongside the rest of a holiday meal.

Ingredients

Use 10–12 cups (about 450–550 g) dried bread cubes; 6 tablespoons butter; 1 medium yellow onion, diced; 3 celery ribs, diced; 2 teaspoons dried sage; 1 teaspoon dried thyme; ½ teaspoon dried rosemary, crushed; ½ teaspoon black pepper; 2–2½ cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth; and 2 tablespoons chopped parsley. Add salt to taste, depending on the broth and bread.

Dry the bread

Cut day-old bread into roughly ¾-inch cubes. Spread on baking sheets and leave uncovered overnight, or bake at 250°F (120°C) for 30–45 minutes, stirring once, until dry but not deeply browned. Dry bread absorbs broth more evenly and helps prevent a soggy stuffing.

Cook the vegetables and herbs

Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and butter a 9-by-13-inch baking dish. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Add onion and celery and cook for 8–10 minutes until softened. Stir in sage, thyme, rosemary and black pepper for about 30 seconds, just until fragrant.

Combine and bake

Place the bread cubes in a large bowl. Pour the vegetable mixture over them and toss. Add 2 cups of broth gradually, tossing until the bread is moist but not swimming in liquid; add more only if dry patches remain. Stir in parsley. Transfer to the baking dish, cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Remove the foil and bake another 15–20 minutes until the top is golden and the centre is hot.

Easy variations

For a richer version, replace part of the broth with sautéed mushrooms. Add cooked diced apple for a sweet contrast, or toasted pecans for crunch. Use vegetable broth for a vegetarian side. If adding cooked sausage, prepare it separately, drain excess fat and mix it through before baking.

Make-ahead plan

Dry the bread cubes several days ahead and store them airtight once fully cool. You can sauté the vegetables a day ahead and refrigerate them. For the best texture, combine the bread and broth shortly before baking; if assembling the whole dish ahead, refrigerate it promptly and expect to add extra baking time straight from cold.

Food safety and storage

For a safer, simpler method, bake stuffing in a separate casserole rather than inside poultry. If stuffing is cooked inside a bird, the centre of the stuffing must reach 165°F (74°C), measured with a food thermometer. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours, use within 3–4 days, and reheat to 165°F (74°C). Follow the USDA food-safety guidance linked below.

Frequently asked questions

What bread works best? A sturdy white, sourdough or country loaf holds its shape well. Why is stuffing mushy? The bread may not have been dry enough or too much broth may have been added. Can I use fresh herbs? Yes; use roughly three times as much fresh as dried, adjusting to taste. Should stuffing be covered? Cover first to heat through, then uncover to crisp the top.

Trusted food-safety guidance

For holiday leftovers, the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service recommends refrigerating perishable food within two hours, using refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days and reheating leftovers to 165°F (74°C). For stuffing cooked inside poultry, use a food thermometer to confirm the centre reaches 165°F (74°C); see USDA temperature guidance.
