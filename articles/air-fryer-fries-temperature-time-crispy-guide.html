Air Fryer Fries: Temperature, Time and Tips for Crispy Results

Make fresh or frozen air fryer fries with practical temperature ranges, timing guidance and simple steps for a crisp outside and fluffy centre.

Publication Today EditorialPublished November 17, 2026

Quick air fryer fries guide

Fresh380°F · 18–22 minFrozen400°F · 10–16 minServings2–3

Times vary by appliance, cut size and basket load. Check the package for frozen fries and cook in a single layer where possible.

Air fryer fries at a glance

For fresh-cut potato fries, start around 380°F (193°C) and allow roughly 18–22 minutes, shaking the basket two or three times. For frozen fries, start with the package directions; many varieties cook in about 10–16 minutes at 400°F (204°C). Air fryer models, cut size and basket load change the result, so treat these as starting points rather than guarantees.

Ingredients for fresh homemade fries

You need 2 medium russet or all-purpose potatoes, about 1 tablespoon of neutral oil, and salt. Optional seasonings include black pepper, smoked paprika, garlic powder or dried herbs. Russets tend to produce a fluffy centre; waxier potatoes hold their shape and have a slightly different texture.

How to make fresh fries in an air fryer

Scrub and cut the potatoes into evenly sized sticks, about ¼ inch thick. For a less starchy surface, soak the cut potatoes in cold water for 20–30 minutes, then drain and dry them very thoroughly. Toss with the oil until lightly coated. Preheat if your model recommends it, arrange the fries in a loose single layer, and cook at 380°F (193°C) for 18–22 minutes. Shake or turn them every 6–8 minutes. Add salt and seasonings after cooking, then serve promptly.

How long do frozen fries take?

Check the bag first because brands and shapes vary. As a practical starting range, cook frozen straight-cut fries at about 400°F (204°C) for 10–16 minutes, shaking halfway through. Crinkle-cut, shoestring and steak fries may need different times. Cook in batches rather than filling the basket deeply; overcrowding traps steam and makes fries softer.

Five ways to get crispier fries

Dry fresh potatoes completely after soaking; use only a light coating of oil; keep the basket in a single layer where possible; shake the basket during cooking; and serve immediately. If the fries are pale or soft, add a few minutes and check again rather than automatically raising the temperature. A little extra space often improves crispness more than extra oil.

Common air fryer fries problems

Soggy fries usually come from excess moisture, overcrowding or uneven pieces. Burnt edges with a soft centre can mean the pieces are too thin or the temperature is too high for the cut. If one side browns faster, shake the basket more often. Different appliances circulate air differently, so note the time that works for your own model.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to soak potatoes? No, but a short soak can help remove surface starch; drying thoroughly afterwards matters. Can you cook fries without oil? Yes, though a small amount can improve browning and texture. Should frozen fries be thawed? No—cook them from frozen unless the package says otherwise. Can you stack fries? A small amount of overlap is manageable, but a crowded basket tends to cook unevenly.

Food handling and serving

Wash fresh potatoes under running water before cutting. Keep raw produce and ready-to-eat foods separate, and clean utensils and surfaces after preparation. For more practical meal ideas, see our guides to a balanced plate and meal prep without spending all Sunday cooking.
