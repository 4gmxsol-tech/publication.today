Champions League Tiebreaker Rules 2026/27: How Teams Are Ranked

How UEFA ranks teams level on points in the 2026/27 Champions League: live Matchday 1–7 criteria, all 10 final tie-breakers, qualification places and the head-to-head rule.

Publication Today EditorialUpdated November 16, 2026
F

Quick answer: During Matchdays 1–7, UEFA ranks clubs level on points by goal difference, goals scored, away goals scored, wins and away wins. If teams remain level after those five criteria, they share the same ranking and are displayed alphabetically by abbreviated name. Once the league phase is complete, all ten criteria in Article 18 apply. Head-to-head results are not a league-phase tiebreaker.

How the 36-team league phase works

All 36 clubs are ranked in one league table. Each team plays eight different opponents: four matches at home and four away. A win earns three points, a draw one point and a defeat zero. The standings remain provisional until every club has completed its eight league-phase matches.

The five criteria used during Matchdays 1–7

While the league phase is in progress, UEFA uses these criteria in order when clubs have the same number of points:

  1. Goal difference: goals scored minus goals conceded across league-phase matches.
  2. Goals scored: the club with more total league-phase goals ranks higher.
  3. Away goals scored: the total number of goals scored in away league-phase matches.
  4. Wins: the club with more league-phase victories ranks higher.
  5. Away wins: the number of victories recorded away from home.

If the clubs are still equal after all five comparisons, UEFA gives them the same ranking and their abbreviated names are displayed alphabetically. That alphabetical order is a presentation rule during the ongoing league phase, not an extra sporting tiebreaker.

All ten final tiebreakers after the league phase

After all eight matches per club are complete, Article 18 applies the full sequence below if teams finish level on points:

  1. Superior goal difference in the league phase.
  2. Higher number of goals scored in the league phase.
  3. Higher number of away goals scored in the league phase.
  4. Higher number of wins in the league phase.
  5. Higher number of away wins in the league phase.
  6. Higher number of points earned collectively by the club’s eight league-phase opponents.
  7. Superior combined goal difference of those opponents.
  8. Higher combined number of goals scored by those opponents.
  9. Lower disciplinary-points total: yellow cards count one point, a red card counts three, and an expulsion for two yellow cards in one match counts three.
  10. Higher UEFA club coefficient.

The opponent-based criteria are only reached if the earlier comparisons do not separate the clubs. Disciplinary points and the club coefficient come later in the sequence, so they are not shortcuts for reading the current table.

Does head-to-head count in the Champions League league phase?

No. A head-to-head result is not a league-phase tiebreaker. The 36 teams do not all play one another, so UEFA uses common table-wide criteria. A direct win still contributes through points, goals and wins, but it does not receive a separate priority over the other criteria.

What the table positions mean

  • 1st–8th: qualify directly for the round of 16.
  • 9th–24th: enter the knockout-phase play-offs.
  • 25th–36th: are eliminated from the competition.

These qualification bands apply to the final league-phase ranking. The current table is a snapshot while matches remain to be played; positions can move after each matchday.

Why the rules matter before Matchday 5

After four matchdays, several clubs are level on nine points. Goal difference, goals scored and the other provisional criteria determine how tied teams are displayed during Matchdays 1–7.
