No-Bake Christmas Treats: 5 Easy Festive Ideas for a Crowd

Make festive no-bake Christmas treats without turning on the oven: chocolate bark, pretzel wreaths, cereal trees, peanut butter bites and dipped pretzels.

Publication Today EditorialPublished November 17, 2026

At a glance

Dessert · About 45–60 minutes including setting time

Ingredients

  • 12 oz semi-sweet or white chocolate
  • Small pretzels and pretzel rods
  • 4 cups mini marshmallows
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 6 cups crisp rice cereal
  • 1 cup smooth peanut butter
  • 2 cups powdered sugar
  • 3 tbsp softened butter
  • Sprinkles, peppermint candies or dried cranberries

Festive sweets without the oven

No-bake Christmas treats are useful when the oven is busy, the kitchen is warm or you want a simple activity with family. These five ideas use easy-to-find ingredients and can be made in small batches or arranged together on a holiday platter. Allow time for chocolate and other coatings to set before serving.

1. Christmas chocolate bark

Line a tray with parchment. Melt 12 ounces (340 g) of semi-sweet or white chocolate gently, using short microwave intervals and stirring between each, or a heatproof bowl over barely simmering water. Spread into a thin layer and scatter with crushed peppermint candies, dried cranberries, toasted nuts or festive sprinkles. Chill until firm, then break into pieces. Keep the toppings dry so the chocolate sets cleanly.

2. Pretzel wreaths

Arrange small pretzels in overlapping circles on parchment. Spoon or pipe melted white chocolate over the joins to hold the wreath shape. Add a few red and green candy pieces or sprinkles before the coating sets. Chill until firm. Make individual wreaths rather than one large ring for easier serving.

3. Cereal marshmallow trees

Melt 3 tablespoons butter in a large saucepan over low heat, add 4 cups mini marshmallows and stir until smooth. Remove from the heat and fold in 6 cups crisp rice cereal. Let the mixture cool until safe to handle, then shape small cones with lightly buttered hands. Decorate with sprinkles or a drizzle of melted chocolate and allow to set.

4. Peanut butter chocolate bites

Mix 1 cup smooth peanut butter with 2 cups powdered sugar and 3 tablespoons softened butter until a firm dough forms. Roll into small balls and chill until firm. Dip each ball in melted chocolate, let excess drip away and place on parchment. Refrigerate until the coating sets. Keep chilled until serving; clearly label these for guests with peanut allergies.

5. Chocolate-dipped pretzel rods

Dip each pretzel rod halfway into melted chocolate, gently tap off the excess and add sprinkles, chopped nuts or crushed peppermint. Place on parchment and let set at cool room temperature or refrigerate briefly. Use separate bowls and utensils for different toppings if you are accommodating allergies.

Plan a simple treat platter

Choose two chocolate-based treats, one crunchy option and one cereal or marshmallow treat for variety. Make bark and peanut butter bites a day ahead, then prepare dipped pretzels and wreaths closer to serving. Use small paper cups or parchment squares to keep sticky items separate. A platter with different shapes and textures looks abundant without requiring a large quantity of each treat.

Storage and allergy notes

Store treats in airtight containers, separating layers with parchment. Follow package storage directions for ingredients such as chocolate, marshmallows and peanut butter. Keep perishable fillings chilled. These ideas contain common allergens including peanuts, milk, wheat and sometimes tree nuts; check every ingredient label and avoid cross-contact when preparing food for guests.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make these ahead? Yes; most can be prepared one or two days ahead if stored correctly. How do I melt chocolate smoothly? Use gentle heat, stir frequently and keep water away from the chocolate. Can I make them nut-free? Choose bark, pretzel wreaths, cereal trees and dipped pretzels, and check labels for nut cross-contact. How long do they take? Most hands-on preparation takes 10–20 minutes per treat, plus chilling or setting time.

Trusted food-safety guidance

For holiday leftovers, the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service recommends refrigerating perishable food within two hours, using refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days and reheating leftovers to 165°F (74°C).
