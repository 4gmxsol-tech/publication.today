Homemade Cranberry Sauce: Easy Recipe with Fresh Cranberries

Make bright, tangy cranberry sauce with fresh or frozen cranberries, sugar and water or orange juice. Includes texture, make-ahead and storage tips.

Publication Today EditorialPublished November 17, 2026

Recipe at a glance

Prep5 minCook10–12 minMakesAbout 2 cups

Simmer 12 oz cranberries with 1 cup sugar and 1 cup water or orange juice. Cool before judging the final thickness.

Why make cranberry sauce at home?

Homemade cranberry sauce takes about 15 minutes of simmering and lets you adjust the sweetness and texture. This small-batch recipe makes roughly 2 cups, enough for a holiday table or several everyday meals. Use fresh or frozen cranberries; there is no need to thaw frozen berries first.

Ingredients

You will need 12 ounces (about 340 g) fresh or frozen cranberries, 1 cup (200 g) sugar, 1 cup (240 ml) water or orange juice, a pinch of salt, and optional orange zest or a small cinnamon stick. Start with the listed sugar, then adjust slightly to taste after the sauce cools.

How to make cranberry sauce

Rinse fresh cranberries under running water and discard any soft or damaged berries. Add the cranberries, sugar, water or orange juice and salt to a saucepan. Bring to a gentle boil over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Reduce the heat and simmer for about 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally. Many berries will pop and the liquid will thicken. Remove from the heat, add orange zest if using, and let the sauce cool. It thickens further as it chills.

How to adjust the texture and sweetness

For a looser sauce, stop simmering when most berries have burst but the mixture still looks slightly fluid. For a thicker, more spoonable sauce, simmer a few minutes longer while stirring. If it tastes too sharp, add a little more sugar while warm. If it is too sweet, add a small splash of orange juice or water and a little zest. Avoid judging the final thickness while the sauce is hot.

Make-ahead and serving ideas

Prepare the sauce a day or two ahead, cool it promptly and refrigerate it in a covered container. Serve chilled or bring it briefly toward room temperature. It works with roast vegetables, sandwiches, oatmeal, yogurt or a cheese board. Leftovers can also be spooned into a simple vinaigrette or used as a fruit layer in breakfast bowls.

Food safety and storage

Wash hands, utensils and work surfaces before preparation. Rinse fresh produce under running water; the FDA does not recommend washing produce with soap or detergent. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within two hours and use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days, following USDA guidance. See the official FDA and USDA resources linked below.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use frozen cranberries? Yes, add them directly to the saucepan and allow a little extra time for the mixture to return to a simmer. Why is the sauce runny? It may need a few more minutes to simmer, and it will thicken as it cools. Can I reduce the sugar? Yes, but the sauce will taste tarter and may have a softer texture. Can it be made ahead? Yes; making it a day or two ahead is convenient.

Trusted food-safety references

For produce preparation, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration guidance on selecting and serving produce recommends rinsing fresh produce under running water and not using soap or detergent. For leftovers, consult the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service guidance.
