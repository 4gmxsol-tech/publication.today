How to Read Health Headlines More Critically

A striking headline is a starting point for questions, not proof that a new treatment or habit works.

Publication Today EditorialUpdated October 2026
✳

Look for the original evidence

Ask whether a headline describes a study in people, animals or cells, and whether it reports an early finding or a well-established result. A press release may simplify details that matter for interpreting the claim.

Check the size and context

A relative percentage can sound dramatic even when the absolute difference is small. Study design, sample size, duration, comparison group and conflicts of interest all affect how much confidence a result deserves.

Separate evidence from advice

One study rarely settles a question. Look for consistent findings across high-quality research and guidance from credible health organizations. Avoid changing medication or treatment based on a headline or social post.

A thoughtful next step

Use this guide as a starting point, not a rigid rulebook. Consider your own circumstances, look for reliable sources, and seek qualified professional advice when a decision affects your health or safety.

Editorial note: Publication Today provides general educational information, not individual medical advice. Health-related decisions should be discussed with an appropriately qualified professional.
