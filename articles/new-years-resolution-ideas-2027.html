50 New Year's Resolution Ideas for 2027 — and How to Keep Them

A good resolution is a small action you will actually do, not a wish you will admire in January and forget in February. Below are 50 ideas for 2027, grouped by area of life, each written the way research says works best — as something to start, with a first step you can take this week. Pick one to three, not fifty.

Publication Today EditorialUpdated November 23, 2026

First, how to pick — and how to word it

Read the list once and mark anything that makes you think "I'd like to be someone who does that." Then narrow it down with three questions:

  • Could I do it on a bad day? If the answer is no, shrink it until the answer is yes. A 10-minute walk survives a bad day; an hour at the gym often does not.
  • Does it have a first step? "Read more" has none. "Read 10 pages before bed" has one — tonight, before bed.
  • Do I know what to do when I slip? Decide in advance: "If I miss a day, then I do the two-minute version the next morning." A plan for the slip is part of the resolution, not an afterthought.

The wording matters more than the topic. In a 2020 study of 1,066 adults, people whose resolutions were about starting something ("walk after lunch", "save every payday") rated themselves successful 58.9% of the time after a year, against 47.1% for resolutions about stopping something. And simply making a resolution helps: in an earlier study, 46% of people who made a New Year's resolution were still succeeding six months later, against 4% of similar people who wanted to change but made no resolution. Every idea below is therefore written as a start-goal with a first step — and the 2027 goals plan shows how to turn one into a full-year system.

Health and movement

Physical health is the most common resolution area, so start here if nothing else calls to you. Each idea is one action, repeated.

  • Walk 10 minutes after lunch on weekdays. First step: set a daily reminder for 12:30.
  • Strength train twice a week for 20 minutes. First step: put two slots in the calendar now.
  • Take the stairs when it is three floors or fewer. First step: use the stairs once today.
  • Stretch for five minutes while the coffee brews. First step: stand in the kitchen tomorrow morning and do it once.
  • Go for one long walk every Saturday morning. First step: pick the route this week.
  • Try one new kind of movement each month — a class, a trail, a sport. First step: look up January's option this weekend.
  • Drink a glass of water with every meal. First step: put a glass on the table at dinner tonight.
  • Keep the same wake-up time seven days a week. First step: set one alarm for the whole week.
  • Do five push-ups before the morning shower — wall or knee push-ups count. First step: do five tomorrow.
  • Walk 8,000 steps a day on average. First step: check tonight's count and write it down.

Money

Money resolutions work best as small, dated actions rather than big targets. Pick one, automate it where you can, and review it monthly.

  • Move a fixed amount to savings every payday, automatically. First step: set up the transfer today.
  • Write down everything spent during January. First step: start a note on your phone tonight.
  • Cancel one unused subscription each month until none are left. First step: list every subscription this week.
  • Wait 48 hours before any unplanned purchase over $50. First step: write the rule on a card in your wallet.
  • Cook dinner at home four nights a week. First step: plan this week's four meals on Sunday.
  • Build a one-month emergency cushion by December. First step: divide the target by 12 and save the first share in January.
  • Check the bank balance every Monday morning. First step: set a Monday 8 a.m. reminder.
  • Plan the week's meals before shopping, and shop with a list. First step: write next week's list before the next shop.

Food and sleep

Food and sleep resolutions fail when they are diets and bans. These are additions and routines instead — things to do, not things to forbid.

  • Eat a vegetable with lunch every day. First step: add one to tomorrow's lunch.
  • Cook one new recipe each month. First step: choose January's recipe now.
  • Eat breakfast sitting down, away from a screen. First step: try it once tomorrow.
  • Pack lunch the night before, three days a week. First step: pack tonight's for tomorrow.
  • Keep the phone out of the bedroom after 10 p.m. First step: charge it in another room tonight.
  • Read 10 pages before picking up the phone at night. First step: put the book on the pillow this evening.
  • Drink water before the first coffee of the day. First step: put a glass by the kettle tonight.

Learning and reading

Learning goals compound quietly. Twelve books, one skill a quarter, ten minutes a day — none of it looks like much in January, and all of it adds up by December.

  • Read 12 books in 2027 — one a month. First step: choose January's book and put it where you will see it.
  • Practise a language 10 minutes a day. First step: open the app after breakfast tomorrow.
  • Take one online class this spring. First step: enrol before the end of January.
  • Listen to one audiobook on the commute each week. First step: download the first one today.
  • Learn one new skill per quarter. First step: write down the four skills now.
  • Write three lines in a journal each night. First step: write three lines tonight.
  • Finish one unfinished course or book from 2026. First step: find it and open it this week.

Relationships

Relationships run on small, repeated contact — a text, a call, a meal. These resolutions are all calendar items, because what is not on the calendar does not happen.

  • Text one friend every Sunday. First step: send the first one this Sunday.
  • Plan one date night a month and put it on the calendar. First step: book January's now.
  • Call one family member each week. First step: choose the day and time.
  • Host one gathering per quarter — even a simple one. First step: pick January's date.
  • Eat one meal a week together, phones away. First step: agree the day with the people involved.
  • Write one thank-you note a month. First step: write January's this week.

Home and organization

Home resolutions work when they are tiny and daily. Ten minutes a day beats a heroic weekend that never happens.

  • Declutter one drawer or shelf each weekend. First step: pick this weekend's drawer.
  • Do a 10-minute tidy every evening. First step: set a 9 p.m. alarm.
  • Make the bed every morning. First step: do it tomorrow before anything else.
  • Follow a weekly cleaning schedule, one room a day. First step: write the seven rooms on a card.
  • Fix one small broken thing per month. First step: make the list of broken things.
  • Clear the inbox to zero every Friday. First step: block 30 minutes this Friday.

Mind and calm

Calm is a practice, not a personality trait. These are short, repeatable pauses that fit into an ordinary day.

  • Sit outside for 10 minutes every morning. First step: take tomorrow's coffee outside.
  • Take three slow breaths before opening email. First step: do it once tomorrow morning.
  • Take one screen-free evening a week. First step: pick the evening and tell the household.
  • Meditate for five minutes after lunch. First step: sit down after lunch tomorrow and time it.
  • Spend 10 minutes a day on a hobby that is not a screen. First step: put the materials where you can reach them.
  • Write down three good things each Sunday night. First step: write this Sunday's three tonight.

How to write your resolution so it sticks

Take your one to three picks and rewrite each one with this formula:

  • Start-goal: "I will walk 10 minutes after lunch on weekdays" — not "I will stop being lazy".
  • First step: the smallest action, dated this week. If the first step takes more than 10 minutes, shrink it.
  • If-then plan: "If I miss a day, then I walk for two minutes the next morning." Across 94 tests, deciding in advance what to do in a difficult situation had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals.
  • Weekly check: one line a week — did it happen, and what gets in the way? Habits take a median of 66 days to become automatic, and missing one day does not derail the process, so the weekly check is where a resolution survives or dies.
  • Tell one person: in the 2020 study, the people who named a support person and got monthly check-ins did best. Say yours out loud to someone who will ask about it.

If your resolutions usually collapse in February, read why resolutions fail by February — and the 90-day fix, then plan only the first quarter with the 2027 goals plan. A plan that covers January to March is a plan you can actually finish — and finishing one quarter is how the year gets finished. Your one-page January plan

Do this on one page, this week, before January arrives:

  • Write your one to three resolutions as start-goals with a first step and an if-then line each.
  • Mark the four 90-day quarters on the 2027 calendar and write one target per goal for Q1 only.
  • Put the weekly actions on specific days — a goal without a day attached waits for a free moment that never arrives.
  • Choose your support person and tell them the plan.

Frequently asked questions

How many New Year's resolutions should I pick for 2027?

One to three. Research on resolutions consistently finds that a small number of specific goals beats a long list. Pick the one or two that matter most, give each a first step you can take this week, and add the rest to a later list once the first ones are moving.

What makes a resolution more likely to last?

Word it as something to start doing, not something to stop. In a 2020 study of 1,066 adults, resolutions about starting a behaviour were rated successful 58.9% of the time after a year, against 47.1% for resolutions about stopping one. Add a visible first step and an if-then plan for the moment you slip.

How long does it take for a new habit to stick?

In a study of habit formation, a new daily habit took a median of 66 days to become automatic, with a wide range of 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the habit. Missing one day did not derail the process, so a single slip is not a reason to quit.

Why do most resolutions fail by February?

The usual reasons are predictable: too many goals at once, goals too vague to act on, and an all-or-nothing rule where one missed day feels like the year is lost. The fix is a 90-day plan with one weekly action per goal and a restart rule — plan the quarter, not the whole year.

Should I write my 2027 resolutions down?

Yes — on one page, where you can see them. Write each resolution as a specific action with a time and a place, add the first step and an if-then line, and review the page weekly. A plan you look at every week is a tool; one you never look at again is a January decoration.

Start with one pick

Do not try to do fifty things in 2027. Pick one idea from this list tonight — the one that made you think "I'd like to be someone who does that" — write its first step, and do that step this week. Next week, add the second. A year built one small action at a time is a year that actually changes.
