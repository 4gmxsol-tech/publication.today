Inclusive Fitness: Making Exercise More Accessible for Everyone

Practical ways to adapt movement, equipment, and exercise environments to different abilities and access needs.

Publication Today EditorialUpdated October 2026
↗

Start with the person, not an assumption

Disability covers a wide range of experiences, and people with the same diagnosis may have different goals and support needs. Ask what activities someone enjoys and what adaptations would help rather than assuming which movements are possible.

Adapt the movement and the environment

Depending on a person's needs, exercise can be performed seated, standing, with support, using resistance bands, or with adapted equipment. Clear routes, accessible changing facilities, suitable surfaces, and instructions in multiple formats can make participation easier.

Build a manageable routine

Choose an activity that feels achievable and progress gradually. Frequency, duration, resistance, and range of motion can be adjusted. Rest breaks are useful tools, not a sign of failure. The best routine is one that fits the person's goals and circumstances.

Make safety individual

Some health conditions or medications affect balance, fatigue, sensation, or cardiovascular response. A qualified exercise professional or healthcare provider can help adapt activity where needed. Stop and seek advice if exercise causes concerning or unusual symptoms.

Accessibility is part of good design

Inclusive programs offer choices, welcome support people when appropriate, and invite feedback from participants with disabilities. Small changes to communication, space, and equipment can remove barriers to regular activity.

A thoughtful next step

Use this guide as general educational information, consider your own circumstances, and consult reliable sources or a qualified professional when a decision affects your health or safety.

Editorial note: Publication Today provides general educational information, not individual medical advice. Health-related decisions should be discussed with an appropriately qualified professional.
