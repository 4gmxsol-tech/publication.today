How to Read a Food Label Without Overthinking It

Start with serving size, ingredients and the nutrients most relevant to you rather than judging a food by one number.

Publication Today EditorialUpdated October 2026
⌁

Check the serving size first

Nutrition numbers are tied to a stated serving, which may differ from what you usually eat. Comparing similar serving sizes helps prevent misleading conclusions when choosing between products.

Use the ingredient list

Ingredients are generally listed by weight, subject to local labeling rules. The list can help identify allergens, added ingredients and the basic composition of a product, but it does not alone determine whether a food fits your needs.

Put numbers in context

A single nutrient value rarely tells the whole story. Dietary needs differ, and foods contribute to overall patterns in different ways. If you manage a medical condition, use advice tailored to that condition.

A thoughtful next step

Use this guide as a starting point, not a rigid rulebook. Consider your own circumstances, look for reliable sources, and seek qualified professional advice when a decision affects your health or safety.

Editorial note: Publication Today provides general educational information, not individual medical advice. Health-related decisions should be discussed with an appropriately qualified professional.
